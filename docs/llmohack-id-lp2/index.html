The more specific customers' questions become, the more it matters who you serve, and with what

With search engines, users mainly look through a list of sites shown for their keywords to find what suits them. With LLMs, users describe their goals, problems and budget in sentences, and get an answer that takes those conditions into account.

For users, this makes it easier to find what fits them. For businesses, it opens up chances to reach more finely divided needs.

To use that chance, you need to work out which needs of which customers your offer can meet, and show on the web, with evidence, how you differ from the businesses you are compared with.

Among finely divided needs, define the areas where you get chosen

For example, in catering in South Jakarta, some offices want “a low-calorie lunch for 20 people, delivered every weekday.” Big brands find small daily office orders hard to prioritize. A small kitchen that delivers to offices and shows the calories of every dish can target that need. It is one example of how fit for a specific customer, rather than size, becomes the reason to be chosen.

When people ask AI, they put these specific situations and conditions into words. So even for searches that compare and select vendors, which affiliate media dominated in traditional SEO, the right approach creates opportunities. Through STP, we choose the areas you serve from many needs, and make clear why you are chosen over competitors.

  • Map the market | Segmentation

    We organize the market by customer attributes, plus goals, problems, usage and selection criteria. With search demand and the voice of customers, we see how needs cluster and how far they spread.

  • Choose your target | Targeting

    We weigh the size of each need, its importance to your business, your assets and the competition, and choose the customers and areas where you can deliver value.

  • Define why you are chosen | Positioning

    We set out your relative strengths against competitors and alternatives, and make clear what value you offer, and to whom.

This makes it concrete which questions you want to be recommended for, and for what reason. We turn customer needs and your value into questions for measurement, and design the actions you need from the gap with today's AI answers.

LLMOHack uses this method to make your value clear, then helps you get mentioned and cited in more AI answers, and win customers who are comparing their options.

  1. STEP 1 | Research customer needs and demand

    From keyword research, monthly search volume and PAA (People Also Ask), plus what you tell us in the form, we learn customers' goals, problems and comparison criteria. Search demand shows the size of each need, PAA widens the picture, and we also capture questions that search terms alone can't express.

  2. STEP 2 | Compare you with competitors, and set your STP

    We compare what competitors and alternatives offer with your products and services, track record, team and tacit know-how, and define your relative strengths. Based on customer needs and demand, we segment the market and set your target and the reason you are chosen.

  3. STEP 3 | Design questions by position, category and goal / problem

    Starting from the customer needs and value set in STP, we design measurement prompts from these angles.

    • Goal / problem: Starting from the customer's problem, does the answer lead to you?e.g. “Lunch for a 20-person office in South Jakarta, any ideas?”
    • Position: When features and conditions that match your strengths are specified, are you recommended?e.g. “Healthy catering that shows calories and delivers to offices?”
    • Category: Are you among the candidates in your product or service category?e.g. “Best healthy catering in South Jakarta?”

    We also check, with questions that name your brand, whether your features and scope are described correctly. All questions are asked in Bahasa Indonesia.

  4. STEP 4 | Show where you are, and the gap

    We look at mentions and citations in AI answers, the reasons given, and which competitors appear. For each thing you want AI to say, we sort out why it is missing: it is written but not picked up, it is not written anywhere, or AI cites your page but its main point does not reach the answer. On the dashboard, we lay out the difference between where you want to be and where you are now.

  5. STEP 5 | Suggest fixes, and check the change every month

    We set priorities by the size of the gap, importance to your business and effort, and suggest what to write, and where. You make the changes, or ask us to, and we ask AI again every month to check what changed.
